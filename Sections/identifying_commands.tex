\section{Identifying commands}


When whisper returns a string of what has been said, the commands need to be extracted from the text on the application layer so the robot can interpret what action has to be taken. This is done by processing the text i.e. Lowercasing all characters, removing special characters like: ``,.!'' and ensuring homophones such as ``hear'' and ``here,'' or other words that sound similar like ``30'' and ``further''. These keywords were found through testing with the different voices and pronounciations the memebers in the group have. All number words under 11 are also converted to numbers, meaning ``one'' becomes ``1,'' as well as SI-units are converted to numbers. Here centimeter would become a factor of 0.01 of the default meter.

As only a limited set of commands are to be expected, problems often associated with natural language transcribing models can be neglected without much consequence. As the whisper model is trained on web data, hallucinations often emerge as words or phrases that are commonly used, such as ``you'' or ``thanks.'' As these words do not correspond to any commands, they can safely be ignored. Furthermore, misrepresentations of words will likely also not correspond to any commands, and can therefore also be ignored.

The extracted command-words are split into different sections: action, direction, distance and unit. An action has to match a direction. This means drive and forward match, however turn and forward do not. Additionally, distance has to match the unit, driving a certain distance is measured in meters, while turning is measured in radians while all other matching units are converted.

An exception is the action ``come here'', the robot triangulates where the person speaking is standing and calculates the amount the robot has to turn and drive to the person speaking. Here, both an angle and distance is calculated, and the robot first turns towards the person, then drives the calculated distance.
