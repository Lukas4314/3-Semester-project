\section{Problem Analysis}
To have a human speak to a robot, and have it understand the commands being said, a microphone is needed to capture the audio input. This makes the sound waves from the human voice into electrical signals that can be processed by the robot. Additionally to have the robot move towards the person speaking, the robot has to be able to locate the direction and distance of the person speaking, in reference to the robot's position. Additional microphones are therefore needed to be able to triangulate the position of the sound source. 3 microphones are chosen to give enough data points for triangulation. By using 3 microphones, and comparing their signals to find the phase difference between them, the direction of the sound source can be estimated. For this, the GCC-PHAT (Generalized Cross-Correlation with Phase Transform) algorithm is chosen, as it offers robust performance in noisy environments. Using the speed of sound, the position can then be estimated by triangulating from the phase differences.

Most microphones often have their seperate clock which they use to sample data, but since the clocks inside the microphones are not perfect they might not produce a clock frequency exactly as required, this results in drifting which causes samples to be taken at different times. For GCC-PHAT to work, the samples needs to be accurate and taken at the same time to ensure that GCC-PHAT does not calculate the phase of the drift, but the phase differnet of the sound waves hitting the microphones at different times, multiple microphones sharing the same clock are used to stay synchronized. I2S (Inter-Integrated circuit Sound) microphones are used as they share the same clock.
For this project a TurtleBot3 Burger robot was supplied, which has a Raspberry Pi, but it can not handle more than one stereo I2S (Inter-Integrated Circuit Sound) audio channel, so an ESP32 microcontroller is needed to interface with the microphones and send the audio data, as it has two stereo I2S interfaces, totaling four mono channels. The microphones can then be mounted on a 3D-printed structure, which provides the necessary fixed geometry for triangulation.


Since a microphone primarily creates small voltage variations in response to sound waves, which can be measured and turned into digital audio data, a way to capture commands from just audio input is required. A reliable and accurate speech recognition system is therefore required, to turn audio data into transcribed text, which later can be analyzed for commands. An AI solution is preferred due to the complexity and variability of human speech. OpenAI's Whisper model is chosen for this task, as it has the required capabilities and is open source, allowing for easy integration and customization. It has also scored at the high end of speech recognition benchmarks, in different models testing their WER (Word Error Rate).


While both the Raspberry Pi and ESP32 are on the robot, neither has the computational power to run the Whisper model in real-time. Therefore, a separate PC will be needed to handle the transcription and sound localization processing. It is important that the PC is equipped with a capable GPU, as it can accellerate the AI model inference significantly, allowing for better real-time performance.

Communication between the PC and the Raspberry Pi will be handled using MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport), a lightweight pub-sub (publish-subscribe) messaging protocol, as it is already in use for command transfer to the robot. The ESP32 does have a Wi-Fi module and can therefore also use MQTT to send the audio data. However, the efficiency and throughput of the Wi-Fi module on the ESP32 might not be sufficient to handle the audio streaming. If that is the case, both the ESP32 and the Raspberry Pi are also equipped with different wired communication protocols, allowing the audio data to be transferred first to the Raspberry Pi, which can then forward it over MQTT to the PC. While dedicated audio streaming protocols like RTP, RTSP, or WebRTC exist, MQTT's simplicity and low overhead make it suitable for this project's needs.
